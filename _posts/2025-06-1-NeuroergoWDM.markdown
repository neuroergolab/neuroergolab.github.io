The <a href="https://neuroergolab.org">Neuroergonomics Lab</a> showcased how VR & AR tools can be used to enrich human-robot interaction at the 2026 <a href="https://widrivesmanufacturing.com">Wisconsin Drives Manufacturing Summit</a>. We showcased an augmented reality (AR) interface, which can visualize the path a robot plans to take, and regions that a human should pay greater attention to. Additionally, we showcased a virtual reality (VR) environment, desinged to train workers on an assembly task, assisted by delivery robots. Both VR and AR tools serve as a low cost option to enrich HRI and training. The development of both projects was lead by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/radiya-firdaus/">Radiya Firdaus</a>, HRI input from <a href="www.linkedin.com/in/spenceleyy">Rob Spenceley</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/nimrobotics/">Aakash Yadav</a>, and general guidance from <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ashanghavi/">Dr. Aditya Shanghavi</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ranjanamehta/">Dr. Ranjana Mehta</a>.
